Document ID: DD-ARCH-014
Author: Ben Okafor, Principal Engineer
Last revised: 03 February 2026
Status: Living document - supersedes DD-ARCH-011

1. PURPOSE

This note describes the shape of the Data Dynamics analytics platform as it stands today, the reasons
behind the main design choices, and the constraints a new service is expected to respect. It is aimed
at engineers who will extend the platform rather than at clients.

2. THE FOUR LAYERS

The platform is organised into four layers: ingestion, storage, transformation and serving. Each layer
is owned by a named team and exposes a contract that the layer above depends on. A change that breaks
a contract requires a design note and a deprecation window of at least one sprint.

2.1 Ingestion
Batch sources land through Airbyte connectors running on our Kubernetes cluster. Streaming sources -
currently the clickstream and the payments event feed - land through Kafka with a three-day retention
window. Every ingested record is stamped with an ingestion timestamp and the source system identifier
before it is written; downstream code must never rely on the source's own event time being trustworthy
without validation, because two of our upstream partners send clock-skewed events.

2.2 Storage
Raw data lands in S3 in Parquet, partitioned by source and ingestion date. Snowflake sits on top as
the analytics warehouse. The bronze/silver/gold convention applies: bronze is an untouched copy of the
source, silver is cleaned and conformed, gold is the modelled layer that analysts and clients query.
Nothing outside the platform team reads from bronze.

2.3 Transformation
All transformation is dbt. Orchestration is Airflow 2.9 running on the same Kubernetes cluster, with
one DAG per data product rather than one enormous DAG. Models are tested with dbt tests; a model
without at least a uniqueness and not-null test on its primary key will fail the CI check.

2.4 Serving
The gold layer is exposed three ways: direct Snowflake access for internal analysts, a REST API for
client applications, and scheduled extracts for clients who still prefer files. The REST API is a
FastAPI service behind our API gateway and enforces per-client row-level filters.

3. SERVICE LEVEL OBJECTIVES

The platform publishes two SLOs that on-call is measured against. Freshness: 95% of gold tables are
refreshed within 4 hours of the source data landing. Availability: the serving REST API returns
successfully for 99.5% of requests measured over a rolling 30-day window.

Freshness is the SLO that breaks most often. The dominant cause is not compute but upstream lateness -
a partner file that arrives late pushes the whole downstream chain past the window. For this reason
DAGs use sensors with explicit timeouts rather than open-ended waits, and a sensor timeout raises an
alert rather than silently retrying forever.

4. COST CONTROLS

Snowflake warehouses are sized deliberately small and are configured to auto-suspend after 60 seconds
of inactivity. The single largest source of unplanned spend in 2025 was a development warehouse left
running by an exploratory notebook, which is why development warehouses now hard-stop after 10 minutes
regardless of activity. Query tags are mandatory: every dbt model and every scheduled query carries a
tag identifying the data product, and untagged query spend is charged to the platform team's budget as
a deliberate incentive.

5. CHOOSING WHERE NEW WORK BELONGS

New engineers frequently ask whether a piece of logic belongs in the ingestion layer or in dbt. The
rule of thumb is that ingestion may reshape but must not interpret. Renaming a column, casting a type
or flattening a nested structure is ingestion work. Deduplicating by business key, applying business
rules or joining across sources is transformation work and belongs in dbt where it is version
controlled, tested and visible in the lineage graph.

6. KNOWN WEAKNESSES

Three weaknesses are acknowledged and tracked on the platform roadmap. First, Kafka retention of three
days is too short to replay a long outage and should move to seven. Second, we have no automated
schema-drift detection on the Airbyte connectors; drift is currently caught by dbt tests failing
downstream, which is late and noisy. Third, the REST API and the scheduled extracts implement
row-level filtering separately, so a filtering rule has to be changed in two places - a consolidation
is planned for Q3 2026.
