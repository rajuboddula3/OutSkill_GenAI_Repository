DATA DYNAMICS - MODEL REPORT: CUSTOMER CHURN PREDICTION v2
Document ID: DD-ML-007
Author: Clara Mendez, Lead Data Scientist
Last revised: 28 January 2026
Model registry entry: churn-propensity/2.1.0
Status: In production for three clients

1. PROBLEM STATEMENT

Subscription clients ask us to identify accounts likely to cancel within the next 60 days so their
retention teams can intervene. Version 1 of the model was a logistic regression trained per client on
a narrow feature set; it performed acceptably but had to be rebuilt from scratch for every new client,
which made onboarding a four-week exercise. Version 2 replaces it with a shared feature specification
and a gradient boosted tree that is fine-tuned per client.

2. DATA AND FEATURES

Training data is drawn from the gold layer, never from bronze or silver, so that the model consumes
the same definitions the client sees on their dashboards. The observation window is 12 months and the
prediction horizon is 60 days.

Features fall into four families:
  - Engagement: sessions per week, days since last login, feature-adoption breadth.
  - Commercial: contract value, plan tier, months since last upgrade or downgrade, discount depth.
  - Support: tickets raised in the last 90 days, median first-response time, count of escalations.
  - Lifecycle: tenure in months, onboarding-completion score, number of seats added or removed.

Days since last login is by a wide margin the strongest single predictor, followed by escalation count
and discount depth. Deeply discounted accounts churn more, not less - discounting tends to be a
symptom of an account already trying to leave rather than a retention device.

3. TRAINING SETUP

The model is a LightGBM classifier. The split is temporal, not random: we train on the first ten
months and validate on the final two. A random split leaks future information through account-level
correlations and inflated our v1 validation scores by roughly nine points of AUC, which is the single
most important methodological lesson from this project.

Class imbalance is around 1:14. We handle it with class weights rather than resampling, because
resampling distorted the calibration of the predicted probabilities and the retention teams use the
probability itself, not just the ranking, to decide how much incentive to offer.

4. PERFORMANCE

On the held-out final two months, pooled across the three production clients:
  - AUC-ROC: 0.847
  - Precision at top decile: 0.61
  - Recall at top decile: 0.44
  - Brier score: 0.071

Per-client AUC ranges from 0.81 to 0.88. The weakest client is the one with the shortest history -
eleven months of usable data - which supports our guidance that we should not promise production-grade
churn scores until a client has at least twelve months of history in the gold layer.

5. FAIRNESS AND GOVERNANCE

The model scores business accounts, not individuals, so it is out of scope for the individual-level
provisions of the client data policy. Nevertheless we exclude any feature derived from the personal
attributes of named contacts, and the feature specification is reviewed by the Data Protection Officer
before any new family of features is added.

Scores are advisory. Client-facing documentation states explicitly that the score must not be used to
withhold service or to price a renewal automatically, and every delivery contract carries that clause.

6. MONITORING AND RETRAINING

Prediction drift is monitored weekly using population stability index on the score distribution; a PSI
above 0.2 opens a ticket. Feature drift is monitored on the top ten features only, which is a
deliberate trade-off to keep the monitoring signal readable.

Retraining is scheduled quarterly and is also triggered on demand if PSI breaches persist for two
consecutive weeks. Every retrain must beat the incumbent on the temporal validation split before it is
promoted; a retrain that does not beat the incumbent is archived and the incumbent stays live.

7. KNOWN LIMITATIONS

The model cannot see anything that happens outside our clients' own systems, so churn driven by a
competitor's pricing move or by an acquisition is invisible to it and shows up as a false negative.
Recall at the top decile of 0.44 means the majority of churners are not in the top decile at all, so
retention teams should treat the score as a prioritisation aid rather than a complete list.
