DATA DYNAMICS - INCIDENT POSTMORTEM
Incident ID: INC-2026-041
Title: Gold layer stale for 19 hours after silent Airbyte connector failure
Severity: SEV-2
Date of incident: 21 February 2026
Author: Ben Okafor
Reviewed by: Anya Raghavan, Clara Mendez
Status: Actions in progress

1. SUMMARY

Between 02:10 and 21:05 UTC on 21 February 2026, the gold layer tables for the Retail Analytics data
product served data that was up to 19 hours stale. Three client dashboards displayed yesterday's
numbers without any indication that the data was old. No data was lost or corrupted, and no
unauthorised access occurred. The cause was a failed Airbyte connector whose failure was not surfaced
because the connector's own retry loop kept the Airflow sensor in a waiting state rather than failing.

2. IMPACT

Two clients noticed and raised tickets before we did, which is the part of this incident we consider
most serious. The Retail Analytics REST API remained available throughout - availability was not
affected - so our availability SLO was met while our freshness SLO was badly missed. Freshness for the
month dropped to 91.4% against a 95% objective. No financial credits were triggered under the client
contracts, but both affected clients requested a written explanation.

3. TIMELINE (all times UTC)

02:10  Upstream partner rotates the credentials on their SFTP endpoint without notifying us.
02:12  Airbyte connector retail_sftp_daily begins failing authentication. It enters its internal retry
       loop, which is configured with unlimited retries and exponential backoff.
02:12  The Airflow sensor waiting on the connector's completion remains in "running", not "failed".
       No alert fires, because our alerting is driven by task failure rather than task duration.
06:00  Morning dbt run executes against unchanged bronze data. All dbt tests pass, because the data is
       internally consistent - it is simply old. Gold tables are rebuilt with yesterday's content.
09:30  On-call engineer performs the morning check, sees a green Airflow view, and moves on.
14:47  First client ticket arrives: "yesterday's sales figures on our dashboard".
16:20  Second client ticket arrives.
17:05  Support escalates to the platform on-call. Investigation begins.
19:40  Root cause identified as the credential rotation and the swallowed connector failure.
20:15  New credentials obtained from the partner and applied to the connector.
21:05  Backfill completes, gold tables refreshed, clients notified.

4. ROOT CAUSE

The direct trigger was an unannounced credential rotation by an upstream partner. The reason a routine
credential problem became a 19-hour incident is that our failure detection is built entirely on task
failure signals. A connector that retries forever never produces a failure signal, so an infinite
retry policy converted a loud, immediate error into a silent, long-running one.

A contributing factor is that dbt tests validate internal consistency but not recency. Every test
passed on stale data because nothing asserted that the maximum ingestion timestamp was recent.

5. WHAT WENT WELL

The backfill itself was clean and took under an hour once the credentials were fixed, which vindicates
the decision to keep raw data in S3 partitioned by ingestion date. Escalation from support to platform
on-call worked as documented and took under twenty minutes.

6. CORRECTIVE ACTIONS

  ACTION-1  Cap Airbyte connector retries at 5 attempts so that persistent failures surface as
            failures. Owner: Ben Okafor. Due: 07 March 2026. Status: done.
  ACTION-2  Add a freshness assertion to every gold model - maximum ingestion timestamp must be within
            the data product's freshness window. Owner: analytics engineering. Due: 21 March 2026.
            Status: in progress, 11 of 18 data products covered.
  ACTION-3  Alert on Airflow task duration exceeding 2x its 30-day median, not only on failure.
            Owner: platform team. Due: 31 March 2026. Status: in progress.
  ACTION-4  Ask all upstream partners to add Data Dynamics to their credential-rotation notification
            lists. Owner: Anya Raghavan. Due: 30 April 2026. Status: not started.
  ACTION-5  Surface a "data as of" timestamp on every client-facing dashboard so staleness is visible
            to the reader. Owner: serving team. Due: 15 May 2026. Status: not started.

7. LESSONS

An unlimited retry policy is not resilience; it is a way of hiding failure. Any wait in our system
must have a timeout, and any freshness objective must have a corresponding automated assertion,
otherwise the objective is only measured after a client complains.
