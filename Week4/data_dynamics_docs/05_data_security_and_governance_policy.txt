DATA DYNAMICS - DATA SECURITY AND GOVERNANCE POLICY
Document ID: DD-SEC-002
Owner: Clara Mendez, Data Protection Officer
Last revised: 15 January 2026
Applies to: All employees, contractors and subprocessors
Review cadence: Annual, or on any material change to a client contract

1. SCOPE AND PRINCIPLES

This policy governs how Data Dynamics handles data belonging to our clients and to their customers.
Three principles run through everything below. Collect the minimum necessary. Grant the least
privilege that allows the work to proceed. Keep data no longer than the contract requires.

2. DATA CLASSIFICATION

Every dataset carries exactly one classification, recorded in the data catalogue at the point of
registration. An unclassified dataset may not be processed.

  PUBLIC       Information already published by the client. No handling restrictions.
  INTERNAL     Operational data with no personal or commercially sensitive content. Accessible to
               all employees on the VPN.
  CONFIDENTIAL Commercially sensitive client data - pricing, contracts, unreleased figures. Access by
               named project team only.
  RESTRICTED   Personal data, payment data, health data, or anything a contract designates as such.
               Access by named individuals, approved by the Data Protection Officer, logged and
               reviewed monthly.

When two datasets are joined, the result inherits the stricter of the two classifications. This rule
is enforced in review, not automatically, so it is the engineer's responsibility to re-classify the
output of a join and update the catalogue entry.

3. PERSONAL DATA HANDLING

Personal data must never leave the client's designated region. Our platform runs in eu-west-1 and
us-east-1; a dataset registered to the EU region is blocked at the storage layer from being copied to
the US region, and any attempt raises a security alert.

Personal data may not be used in development or test environments. Where realistic test data is
needed, use the synthetic generation utility in the platform monorepo, which preserves distributions
without reproducing real records. Masking production data is not an acceptable substitute, because
masked data has repeatedly proved re-identifiable when joined against other datasets.

Exports of RESTRICTED data require a documented business reason, an approval from the Data Protection
Officer, and an expiry date. Exports without an expiry date are automatically revoked after 30 days.

4. ACCESS CONTROL

Access is granted through Okta groups and is always role-based. Individual grants on a warehouse
object are prohibited; if you find one, report it, because it is invisible to our quarterly access
review and will drift out of date.

Production write access requires two approvals, completion of the Data Handling course, and is
reviewed every quarter. Access that has not been used for 90 days is revoked automatically. Service
accounts are exempt from the 90-day rule but must have a named human owner, and an unowned service
account is disabled at the next review.

5. SECRETS AND CREDENTIALS

Credentials live in the secrets manager and nowhere else. A credential committed to a repository is a
reportable security incident even if the repository is private and even if the commit is amended away,
because the credential must be assumed compromised and rotated.

Client credentials for upstream systems are held per-client and are never shared across engagements.
Rotation is every 90 days, and the platform team maintains the rotation calendar.

6. RETENTION AND DELETION

Retention periods are set per client in the engagement contract. Where a contract is silent, the
default is 24 months from collection for INTERNAL and CONFIDENTIAL data and 12 months for RESTRICTED
data. Retention is implemented as an automated lifecycle rule on the S3 partitions and a corresponding
Snowflake task; retention that exists only in a document and not in a lifecycle rule is not retention.

On contract termination, client data must be deleted within 30 days and a deletion certificate issued.
Backups are covered: our backup retention is 35 days, and the certificate states that residual copies
in backup will age out within that window rather than claiming immediate erasure.

7. INCIDENT REPORTING

Any suspected exposure of client data must be reported to the Data Protection Officer within one hour
of discovery, through the #dd-security-incident channel or directly by phone out of hours. Do not
attempt to assess severity before reporting - report first, assess second. Under GDPR we may have as
little as 72 hours to notify a supervisory authority, and that clock starts at discovery, not at the
point we finish investigating.

Reporting a suspected incident in good faith never attracts blame, including when the reporter caused
it. Failing to report one does.
